Not every student learns music by sitting still, reading notes and following the same book.

At Ears & Keys, the lesson adapts to the person. We may work through sound, imitation, movement, patterns, improvisation, visual supports, favorite music, traditional notation — or a combination of all of them.

The Ears & Keys approach

Hear it. Understand it. Make it yours.

01

Listen first

Students learn to notice pulse, structure, patterns, bass, harmony, melody and musical color — not just which key to press.

02

Learn through more than one sense

Sound, movement, touch, visual cues and pattern recognition can all become pathways into the music.

03

Build real musicianship

Technique and reading still matter, but they grow alongside playing by ear, improvising, understanding music and expressing something personal.

Neurodivergent learners

There is more than one way to participate in music.

I welcome autistic, ADHD and other neurodivergent students, including students who communicate or move differently.

The goal is not to make every student look like a conventional piano student. We start by discovering what creates connection: a sound, a rhythm, a favorite song, a repeated pattern, movement, touch, a visual cue, or simply enjoying being at the instrument.

Ears & Keys provides music education, not music therapy. I am a piano educator who is continuing to deepen my training in adaptive and neurodiversity-affirming teaching.

“Progress can mean playing a song. It can also mean making a choice, anticipating a sound, joining a rhythm, reaching for the piano — or simply wanting to come back.”

Meet Jordane

Pianist. Teacher. Listener.

I began piano as a young child in the conservatory system and have spent years teaching students with very different goals, personalities and ways of learning.

Over time, I became less interested in teaching students to simply reproduce the “right” notes and more interested in helping them actually hear, understand and connect with music. Ears & Keys grew from that idea.

My teaching blends strong piano foundations with ear training, pattern recognition, improvisation, movement, creativity and individualized learning.
